01 — Welcome

Welcome to Prolific

Why the Bison?

The symbol of Prolific is the bison for a reason. The bison, upon sensing an approaching storm, charges directly into it rather than running away. By running straight through the storm, the bison minimizes the amount of time it spends exposed to harsh conditions, ultimately getting through the storm more quickly and with less suffering than if it had tried to outrun it or drift along with it.

At Prolific, we build championship DNA. We are the bison. We run towards discomfort and choose to do hard things. We do what most won't, and as a result, we achieve what most can't.

Core Values

  1. Extreme Ownership: Victim culture will not be tolerated. At Prolific we take full ownership over our actions and results because that is the path to growth and freedom.
  2. FITFO: FITFO means "figure it the f*** out." Prolific is a culture of problem solvers and solution seekers. When in doubt, figure it out.
  3. Discipline: Motivation is temporary. At Prolific we do what is required and will help us reach our goals regardless of how we feel. A culture of discipline is the only way we can all achieve our goals.
  4. Gratitude: We always have a reason to be grateful. Even when we're in the eye of the storm, life is good.

04 — Advancement

How Pay Works

Your commission per install is determined by two factors: your rank tier, which sets your pay scale, and the speed package the customer purchases. Every ISP operates across three provider tiers — Tier I, Tier II, and Tier III — and within each tier, pay varies based on the speed sold. Faster packages pay more. Higher ranks pay more. Both compound together. The four speed categories across all tiers are: greater than 1G, 1G, 500 MB, and less than 500 MB.

Your base pay scale is anchored to your Tier I, 1G commission rate. Everything else on the pay table adjusts up or down from that anchor based on the tier of the ISP you're selling and the speed package the customer selects. Always know what tier your current ISP falls under and always pitch the highest speed package the customer qualifies for — every upsell matters.

05 — Getting Paid

Pay Schedule & How You Get Paid

Understanding your pay schedule is not optional — it directly affects how you manage your money, plan your finances, and catch errors before they cost you. Read this section carefully and refer back to it whenever you have questions about a payment.

How Payroll Works

All commissions at Prolific are processed through Everee via direct deposit. After you complete onboarding with The League, you will receive an email with your Everee account creation link. You must complete this setup before you can receive payment — do not wait on it. Getting your Everee account set up on day one is non-negotiable. Reps who delay this step delay their first paycheck.

Payroll always lands on Friday. Every Wednesday you will receive a pay report showing exactly what is hitting your account that Friday. Review this report every single week. If something looks wrong, Wednesday is your window to catch it and get it resolved before pay day. Do not wait until Friday and then wonder why your check is short — by then there is nothing anyone can do until the following week.

The M1/M2 Split

Your commission on every install is paid in two parts. M1 is 85% of your commission and is paid upfront on your normal pay schedule after the install completes. M2 is the remaining 15% and is paid 100 days after the date your M1 payment was received.

This structure exists because of the 90-day chargeback window. The M2 payment is your commission being held until the customer's account is confirmed stable. Once 100 days have passed from your M1 payment, that remaining 15% releases to you automatically. This is not money that disappears — it is money that is yours as long as the customer stays active. Think of it as a built-in savings mechanism tied to your performance.

The 90-Day Chargeback Window

If a customer cancels their service within 90 days of install completion, you will receive a chargeback on that account. This means the M1 commission you were paid for that install will be deducted from a future paycheck. The M2 portion is simply withheld and never paid out.

Why compliance matters

Pitching free trials, making promises outside of what the ISP offers, or rushing customers who aren't fully committed all increase your cancel rate and your chargeback exposure. Every cancellation is a double hit — you lose the original commission and you get the M1 clawed back. Protect your earnings by selling customers who are genuinely sold. A sale that cancels in 60 days isn't a sale. It's a liability.

Pay Schedules by ISP

The timing of when your install converts to a paycheck varies by ISP. Here is the complete breakdown for every provider Prolific works with.

Standard — paid 2 Fridays after install week

If your install completes during a given week, you are paid two Fridays later. These ISPs all follow the same cadence:

Astound · Frontier · AT&T · Bluepeak · Omni · Optimum · Tru Vista · Verizon · Wire 3 · Xfinity

Brightspeed — Exception

Brightspeed pays 1 Friday after funding. Funding typically occurs 1–2 weeks after install completion, which means your total wait time from install to pay is roughly 2–3 weeks depending on when funding processes. You are paid on the Friday following the funding confirmation.

Kinetic — Exception

Kinetic follows a biweekly pay cycle rather than weekly. The same 2+ week post-install timeline applies, but instead of every Friday, Kinetic payments process every other Friday. If you are running a Kinetic blitz, plan your finances accordingly — you will not see a Kinetic paycheck every week.

Ting — Exception

Ting pays 3 weeks after sale confirmation rather than 2 weeks after install completion. The starting point is also different — it is based on confirmation of the sale, not the install date. Factor this in when projecting your income on Ting markets.

Payroll Issues & Disputes

If your Wednesday pay report shows something missing or incorrect, do not wait until Friday hoping it works itself out. Text the League One Stop support line at 866-605-5076 and start your message with the word "PAYROLL." Support hours are Monday–Friday, 9am–5pm Mountain Time.

For anything that requires escalation beyond the One Stop line, contact the Prolific Support Line at 949-827-9851, available Monday–Saturday, 9am–8pm Mountain Time. Do not let a pay issue sit. Address it immediately, use the right channel, and follow up if you don't hear back within 24 hours.

06 — Foundation

The 7 Laws of D2D

These are the foundational principles that separate reps who grind it out and go home with nothing to show for it from the ones who build real careers and real income. Study these. Internalize them. Come back to them when things get hard. The 7 Laws aren't motivational content — they are operating principles.

Law 1

Law of Clarity

“All achievement begins with knowing exactly what you want and focusing your energy on that single, unwavering objective.”

Most reps show up to the doors with a vague idea of what they want. They say things like "I want to make good money this summer" or "I want to do well." That's not a goal — that's a wish. The Law of Clarity demands more than that.

Clear Goal

Write your goals out as clearly and specifically as possible. Have one chief aim — the single most important outcome you are working toward. Make it visible. Put it on a goal board, set it as your phone screensaver, write it on paper and read it every morning. A goal that lives only in your head is easy to forget when things get hard. Making it visible makes it real. The reps who hit their goals aren't smarter or more talented than the ones who don't — they simply had a clearer picture of what they were working toward and looked at it every day.

Clear Path

Knowing what you want is only half of it. You also need to understand exactly what it will take to get there. Break it down through reverse engineering. If you want to install 100 accounts this quarter, work backwards — how many sales does that require per week? Per day? How many doors do you need to knock to generate that many sales based on your current ratio? Blind goals are empty dreams. The rep who has a clear goal and a clear path to it walks out the door every morning with a completely different energy than the rep who is just "going out to knock."

Law 2

Law of Averages

“If you do something enough, a ratio will begin to appear.”

D2D is a numbers game. The rep who understands this never has a truly bad day — they only have days that are building toward their ratio. The rep who doesn't understand it gets emotionally destroyed by rejection and quits before the numbers ever get a chance to work in their favor. Rejection in D2D is not personal — it is statistical. Every door that doesn't sell is part of the equation that makes the door that does sell possible.

Find the Ratio

Over your first 30 days, track every KPI — doors knocked, conversations had, speed tests run, sales made, installs completed. Use a tally app to track doors, conversations, and speed tests in real time. Use the League Fiber App to track sales and installs. After 30 days, your ratio will start to reveal itself. You'll know exactly how many doors it takes to get a conversation, how many conversations it takes to close a sale, and how many sales convert to installs. That ratio is your baseline.

Use the Ratio

Once you have your ratio, it becomes your roadmap. Match your daily door goals to your install targets. If you know it takes 80 doors to close 2 sales and you want 3 sales today, you know you need to knock at least 120 doors. It's math, not luck. Keep tracking your KPIs as you train and develop your skills, and watch the ratio improve over time. Your ratio should be improving every single month. If it isn't, your training isn't working.

Law 3

Law of Intentionality

“Personal growth and success do not happen by accident.”

The gap between where most people are and where they want to be is not a lack of talent or opportunity. It's a lack of intention. Most people are drifting — going through the motions, existing, reacting to whatever the day brings them. Drifters guarantee failure. By default, we are either growing or dying. If you are not actively getting better, you are getting worse. There is no neutral.

Drifting

Drifting looks like showing up to the doors without a plan, skipping training because you're tired, letting distractions eat your prime hours, and telling yourself you'll be more focused tomorrow. Drifters find reasons. High performers find ways. The dangerous thing about drifting is that it feels comfortable. You're still showing up. You're still knocking on doors. You just aren't being intentional about any of it — and the results reflect that every time.

Structure

Intentionality requires structure. Weekly planning sessions, daily training, and a locked-in schedule for the doors are not optional for serious reps — they are the baseline. Build habits that support your growth and development. Eliminate distractions during your working hours. Intention without structure is just motivation — and motivation fades. Structure is what makes intention durable.

Law 4

Law of Consistency

“You must first become consistent before you can become exceptional.” — Hormozi

Intensity gets attention. Consistency builds careers. You will have big days — days where everything clicks and you close deal after deal. And you will have slow days where nothing seems to work. The Law of Consistency says that neither of those days matters as much as what you do every single day in between.

Compound Effect

Your actions compound over time. The first phase of anything is always the slowest and the least rewarding — this is where most people quit. Think about a penny doubled every day for 30 days. On day 10 you have $5.12. On day 20 you have $5,242. On day 30 you have over $5 million. The explosive growth only happens because of everything that came before it. Do not quit before the compounding kicks in.

Inertia

An object in motion stays in motion. Consistency breeds more consistency. Once you build a rhythm — a daily routine, a knocking habit, a training practice — keeping it going is far easier than starting it fresh after a break. This is why we live by the rule of no more than one day off. Taking two days off doesn't just cost you two days of sales. It costs you momentum, and momentum is one of the most valuable assets in D2D.

Law 5

Law of Adaptability

“All failure is the failure to adapt; all success is successful adaptation.” — Max McKeown

D2D is unpredictable. Markets change. ISP promotions change. Neighborhoods are different. Customers are different. Weather happens. Plans fall apart. The rep who needs everything to be perfect in order to perform will never reach their potential. The rep who can adapt to anything is unstoppable.

Expect Challenges

Things will go wrong. That is not a possibility — it is a certainty. The rep who plans for adversity doesn't get thrown off balance when it arrives. Prepare for the bad days, the rough neighborhoods, the slammed doors, the ISP system outages, and the canceled installs. When you expect things to be easy and they aren't, you break. When you expect things to be hard and they are, you adapt.

Do or Do Not

There is no "try" at Prolific. Saying you tried gives you an out — a way to feel okay about not getting the job done. We either get it done or we don't. Be the rep who figures it out regardless of what the day throws at them. The question is never "why isn't this working?" The question is always "what do I need to change to make this work?"

Law 6

Law of Ownership

“When a team takes ownership of its problems, the problem gets solved.” — Jocko Willink

The single most common trait of underperforming reps is blame. The neighborhood was bad. The leads were bad. The weather was bad. My manager didn't help me enough. The Law of Ownership eliminates blame entirely — not because those things aren't sometimes true, but because blame never closes a deal. Blame is a defense mechanism that protects your ego at the cost of your growth.

Take Responsibility

Ownership means taking responsibility for finding solutions rather than assigning blame. When something goes wrong, the first question is not "whose fault is this?" The question is "what could I have done differently to produce a better outcome?" That question is where growth lives. The moment you stop asking what you could have done differently is the moment you stop improving.

Accountability

Ownership is contagious. The level of accountability you bring to your work becomes the standard for the people around you. High-ownership reps build high-ownership teams. Low-ownership reps drag everyone down with them. Every morning you walk out the door, you are setting the standard for the people watching you. Choose the standard you want to set.

Law 7

Law of the Lid

“Success is not limited by opportunity as much as it is by leadership.”

This law applies to every rep, not just managers. Your leadership ability — the way you lead yourself, manage your mindset, and influence the people around you — sets a ceiling on how far you and your team can go. You can have the best market, the best ISP, the best promotion, and the best manager in the organization — and still underperform, if your leadership of yourself is weak.

What's Your Lid

Your leadership ability determines how effective you and everything connected to you can become. A rep who leads themselves well — who shows up on time, trains daily, manages their emotions under pressure, and holds themselves to a high standard — raises the ceiling for their own results. Your lid is not fixed. But it does limit you until you raise it.

Raise Your Lid

The good news is that leadership is a skill, not a trait. It can be developed. When leaders improve their ability to communicate clearly, build trust, delegate effectively, develop the people around them, and make confident decisions, the performance of the entire team rises with them. Invest in your leadership. Read. Train. Study the reps and managers who are outperforming you and figure out why. Raise your lid and watch your ceiling disappear.

07 — Product

Introduction to Fiber

Fiber internet is a high-speed internet connection that uses fiber-optic cables — thin strands of glass or plastic — to transmit data as pulses of light. This allows information to travel much faster and more reliably than through traditional copper cables used in DSL or cable internet. They have been running these fiber lines throughout neighborhoods for years. Our job is to go to those neighborhoods and get those homes connected to the line that goes down the street. Each home we sell will get their own dedicated fiber line to the master line.

"Drops" are the verbiage for when the home is connected to the main fiber line that will run down their street. This usually follows the same type of connection as the home's power lines. If the home's power lines are underground or overhead, the fiber lines will match that.

Why Fiber? Currently through Coax (Copper) lines, our nation's infrastructure doesn't have unlimited bandwidth and the lines have to be relaid every time we want to upgrade. With Fiber, we never have to relay the lines and every home will get symmetrical speeds for download and upload.

Faster Speeds

Fiber is capable of delivering gigabit or multi-gigabit speeds for both downloads and uploads. This is not a marginal improvement over what most customers currently have — it is a generational leap in capability.

Greater Reliability

Fiber is far less affected by weather or distance than copper-based internet, offering a more stable and consistent connection. The outages and slowdowns customers experience during peak hours or bad weather are largely a result of the limitations of the infrastructure they are currently on.

Low Latency

Fiber is ideal for video calls, gaming, and streaming where real-time response matters. The lag customers experience on Zoom calls and FaceTime is a direct result of upload speed limitations that fiber solves completely.

Future-Ready

Fiber can handle growing data demands as technology continues to evolve. Unlike copper infrastructure, fiber does not need to be replaced as speeds increase — the same lines can carry exponentially more data as technology improves. The customer who gets on fiber today is not just solving their current problem. They are future-proofing their home.

08 — The Pitch

The Pitch & Script

The Skeleton of the Pitch

Every ISP, every market, and every promotion will be different. But the structure of a great pitch never changes. Master these six steps and you will have a repeatable system that works regardless of what door you're standing at.

Step 1

Intro

The goal of the intro is to get their attention, establish who you are, and clearly explain why you're working in their neighborhood. You have about 5–10 seconds before the customer decides whether to engage or shut you down. Lead with confidence, a genuine smile, and a clear reason for being there. The customer needs to understand two things immediately: who you are and why you're there. Keep it simple. Your only job in the intro is to earn the next 30 seconds of their attention.

Step 2

Discovery

Before you pitch anything, you need to gather information. This is not small talk — it is intelligence gathering that will make every subsequent step of your pitch more effective and more personal. Find out three things:

  • Who do they currently use for their internet? This tells you who you're competing against and what the customer's baseline experience has been.
  • What other services are they bundling with that provider? Understanding what they're bundled into tells you how sticky their current situation is and whether there's a consolidation angle.
  • How much are they currently paying? Get a number. When you ask them to pull up a recent bill, the number is almost always higher than they thought. That moment of surprise is your ally.

Listen more than you talk during this step. The answers the customer gives you here are the foundation for everything that comes next.

Step 3

Build Pain

The goal of this step is not to talk negatively about their current provider. It's to surface the frustrations they've likely already experienced but haven't had a reason to act on. Reference what you've been hearing in the neighborhood — slow or inconsistent speeds, prices that have crept up year over year, long hold times, frequent outages. Then ask if any of that sounds familiar. Most of the time it will.

After that, have them run a speed test. Ask them to grab their phone and type "wifi speed test" into Google. The blue box will run the test instantly. What they see on that screen — especially their upload speed — almost always does the work for you. Let the data build the pain.

Step 4

The Hook

Now that the customer feels the gap between what they're paying and what they're getting, this is where you fill it. The hook is your promotion — delivered clearly, with confidence, and tailored directly to what matters most to this specific customer. Build value around the benefits — symmetrical speeds, dedicated fiber line, reliability — then present the price and any incentives. The key to a great hook is hitting their hot button — the specific pain point that resonated most in step three.

Step 5

Show Plans

This is where you step into the friend zone. Move shoulder to shoulder with the customer and present the plan on your tablet or phone as if you're two people looking at something together. Present the plan that makes the most sense for them specifically — don't dump every available option on them and create decision fatigue. Use your discovery to guide your recommendation. Keep this step simple and conversational.

Step 6

Assume the Sale

Do not ask the customer if they want to sign up. Assume they do. The close is not a question — it is a continuation of the conversation that began the moment they opened the door. "What day works better for you — Thursday or Friday?" is how you close. "So would you like to move forward?" is a hesitation. A customer who is still standing there at step six is almost always a customer who just needs you to take the wheel.

Example Pitch

Rep: Hey, how's it going? I'm _____ with _____, the fiber company. I'm sure you've noticed our trucks out here — we've been running fiber lines through the neighborhood over the last several months and now we're coming around connecting homes to the line that runs down your street.

Rep: Quick question before I show you what we're doing out here — who are you guys with for internet right now?

Customer: Xfinity.

Rep: Got it. And are you bundling anything with them — TV, a phone line, anything like that?

Customer: No, just internet.

Rep: And do you know roughly what you're paying a month for that?

Customer: I think around $90 or so. Maybe more, I'm not totally sure.

Rep: Okay, perfect.

— Build Pain —

Rep: So the reason we're upgrading the lines in your neighborhood is that a lot of people have been dealing with the same things — speeds that are inconsistent, especially in the evenings, and then Xfinity keeps bumping the bill up every year without giving you anything extra for it. Have you guys dealt with any of that?

Customer: Yeah honestly the speeds have been pretty bad lately, especially at night.

Rep: That's exactly what everyone's been saying. It's the old copper infrastructure — it's a shared line, so the more people using the internet in your area at once, the slower it gets for everyone. Here, grab your phone real quick and type “wifi speed test” into Google for me — the blue box will run it right there.

Customer: Okay hold on… it says 147 download and 8 upload.

Rep: See that upload speed — 8 megabits. That's why if you've ever been on a FaceTime or a Zoom call and it's pixelated or keeps cutting out, it's your upload speed. You're paying around $90 a month for that. Let me show you what we're doing for your neighbors.

— The Hook —

Rep: So while we're out here, we're getting people set up on a full gigabit of fiber — that's a thousand download and a thousand upload, for $__ a month locked in for a full year. And since we're already out here connecting the lines, the install and all the equipment is completely free. No technician fee, no equipment rental, nothing. Cheaper price & faster speeds.

— Show Plans — step shoulder to shoulder, pull up the plan —

Rep: So this is what I've been setting everyone up with out here. One full gig — a thousand up and a thousand down — locked in at $_____ a month for the first year. No contracts, free install, all new equipment included. Based on what you're paying now and what you're getting, it's a no-brainer upgrade. I just set your neighbor up with this same plan two doors down.

— Assume the Sale —

Rep: We've got installs going in this area Thursday and Friday. What's your last name so I can pull up our install schedule?

Customer: It's Johnson.

Rep: And the best number to reach you at for the install confirmation?

Customer: 555-whatever.

Rep: Morning or afternoon work better for you on Thursday?

Customer: Morning is better.

Rep: Morning it is. Let me grab your address to complete the order and we'll get you all set.

Tailoring Your Pitch

The most successful reps at Prolific use local knowledge mixed with the promotion to tailor their pitch to the homeowner's specific needs. If you're knocking in a small city that has bad reception, customers' concerns may be around having a consistent signal or frequent outages rather than the highest possible speeds. If you're in an area with a lot of remote workers, the focus should be on upload speed and video call reliability. If you're in a neighborhood with a lot of families, streaming and gaming speed for multiple devices at once is the angle.

For example, in an area with known outage issues: "We're out here helping more people get set up with fiber to avoid the outages your neighborhood has been experiencing. Do you guys have outages more during bad weather or does it happen more frequently?"

Tailoring your pitch gives you validity — you know the situation there — and gives you genuine confidence that what you're selling actually benefits this specific homeowner. What's important is that you become the "Fiber Guy" in your neighborhood. Whether you make the sale or not, don't be afraid to ask homeowners questions to get to know the area. The more you know about the neighborhood, the more authentic and effective your pitch becomes.

09 — In the Field

Area Management & Schedule

Area Management

Area management is vitally important in your success, your relationship with the ISP, and the overall success of your blitz. When you're given an area it will be on the door knocking app provided by the ISP. Your area will show pins on homes that are serviceable and won't show pins on homes that are already customers or aren't serviceable.

You should always have a plan at the beginning of the day on where you'll be and what you'll be doing the entire time. As you work your neighborhood in the morning, only hit homes with signs of life — cars in the driveway, window shades open, and other hints that people are possibly home. If someone isn't home, take notes of what cars are there. This will give you more signs of life when you walk past in the evening and see another car has appeared.

The worst mistake rookies make is not taking good notes. You'll find yourself walking around the neighborhood confused about where you've been or who you've spoken to. As you take good notes, the days following in that same area will have more purpose. You'll be able to plan out the exact homes to follow up with and name drop the sales you got in previous days, which gives you validity and makes you the "fiber guy" in that area. Homeowners talk to each other. When they hear their neighbor already signed up, your job gets easier.

ISPs check our activity. The better the notes, the more sales per neighborhood. Making a plan is key to making the most out of an area. Plan before you go, plan the first 10 doors you'll knock, and take good notes throughout the entire day.

Daily Schedule

"Proper planning prevents poor performance."

The following is an example of a productive daily schedule. Your specific hours may vary by market and manager, but the principles of how to structure your time within those hours apply universally.

11am – 3pm

Signs of Life & Early Deals

During this window you'll find a lot of older folks home as well as people who work from home who need good internet. Set a goal for sales before 3pm. Selling 1, 2, or 3 deals before 3pm lets you take a deep breath going into the later part of the day. An extra sale every day before 3pm adds 20–30% more to your total income for the year. If a non-decision maker needs to talk to their partner, schedule a callback for the end of the day — it gives you more chances for late-night deals that many reps miss.

4pm – 8pm

Prime Time

The most important selling window of the day. People are getting home from work and decision makers are finally home. This is where 70% of your sales will happen. You should be pushing through fresh areas you haven't hit yet — saving your best untouched territory for prime time is a legitimate strategy. Do not use this window for callbacks. Every minute between 3pm and 8pm should be spent finding people you haven't spoken to yet.

8pm – 9pm

Callbacks & Finishing Strong

You're tired. You've been working all day. None of it is a reason to go home. Another 20–30% of your daily business comes from working after dark. The people you're visiting during this window are the ones least spoken to by other reps, and the non-decision makers from earlier whose decision maker is now home. Don't be the rep who goes home at 7pm and wonders why their numbers are always just short of their goals.

10 — Standards

Compliance & Communication

While you're selling fiber you are representing the ISP you're selling for and Prolific. Everything you do in the field reflects on both organizations. Do not do anything that would hurt either's reputation.

Take this seriously

If you break compliance standards with any ISP it is possible that they will ban you from selling with them permanently. A single compliance violation can end your ability to work with a provider entirely — and in some cases, that information follows you to other ISPs as well. Compliance is the foundation of trust between Prolific, The League, and our ISP partners. Without that trust, there are no blitzes, no markets, and no opportunities.

Every ISP has their own specific compliance standards. The following are the key rules that are standard across all providers:

  • Wear your ISP gear and badge at all times in the field. If you don't have what you need, speak with your manager before going out. Showing up at a door without proper identification creates immediate distrust.
  • Do not ever pitch a free trial at the door. For you to receive your full commission and avoid a chargeback, the customer needs to stay active for 90 days. Pitching a free trial attracts customers who have no intention of keeping the service.
  • Act professionally with all customers at all times. This includes walking away from arguments, never acting out of emotion, and never insulting or belittling a customer. No sale is worth a compliance report. Walk away, reset, and move to the next door.
  • Never use your personal information while signing up a customer — including your personal email if the customer doesn't have one. Always use the appropriate fields and protocols provided by the ISP's ordering system.
  • Refrain from promising anything outside of what the ISP officially offers. Back-door promises — discounts, add-ons, install guarantees that aren't part of the official offer — hurt the ISP's reputation and create expectations that will never be met. When a customer calls to complain, that complaint comes back to us.
11 — Delivery

Non-Verbal Skills

What you don't say matters as much as what you do say. Non-verbal communication is happening from the moment the door opens — before you say a single word, the customer is already forming an opinion about whether you are trustworthy, confident, and worth listening to.

Smile

Look sincere. Smile with your eyes, not just your mouth. A smile is your greatest asset — it releases endorphins in both you and the customer and creates an immediate sense of warmth and openness. A forced smile reads as fake. Practice smiling genuinely until it becomes natural.

Head Nod

Use full head movement, focusing on moving your chin up and down. Wait for a confirmation — a small "yes" from the customer — before moving on. Every nod the customer returns is a micro-commitment that makes the eventual close easier.

Posture

Stand up straight with your shoulders back. This is one of the single biggest indicators of confidence. Slouching signals insecurity. Good posture signals that you belong there.

Eye Contact

Maintain solid eye contact throughout your pitch. Looking away too much signals dishonesty or nervousness. Too much right at the start can feel confrontational. Plan your natural look-aways and return to solid eye contact consistently.

Body Position

Stand on the hinge side of the door, almost completely sideways, so they can see the walkway and feel that you're not blocking their exit. Put your weight on your front foot to signal that you are present and attentive. Never square your shoulders directly to the customer — that reads as confrontational.

Slow Movements

Look up slowly when the customer opens the door. When you point at the logo on your shirt or at anything in the environment, move extremely slowly. Fast movements look nervous. Slow movements communicate confidence and intentionality.

Statue Stance

Stand completely still. No shuffling feet, no shifting weight, no fidgeting. Unplanned, unconscious movements make you look nervous. The stillness itself communicates that you are completely comfortable where you are.

12 — Delivery

Para-Verbal Skills

Para-verbal refers to how you say what you say — your volume, speed, tone, and pausing. These elements often carry more weight than the actual words of your pitch. You can have the most perfectly crafted script in the world and blow it entirely with the wrong delivery.

Volume

Speak softly and calmly. Match the customer's volume naturally. If they whisper because a baby is sleeping, you whisper too. Matching volume is one of the fastest ways to build unconscious rapport.

Speed

Speak very slowly. This is one of the most common mistakes rookies make — talking too fast. Fast talkers make customers nervous and come across as pushy. When something is important to us, we slow down and emphasize. It also gives the customer time to absorb what you're telling them.

Pauses

Silence is golden — especially after head nods while waiting for a customer's confirmation. Confident people are completely comfortable with silence and use it intentionally. Amateur reps talk through their closes. Experienced reps pause after them and let the silence do the work.

Down Tones

Finish your sentences with your vocal tone going down. An upward inflection makes it sound like a question — like you're seeking approval. A downward inflection signals confidence, knowledge, and authority.

Tone Matching

Mirror the customer's energy and personality. If they're cheerful and warm, match that. If they're serious and no-nonsense, be more direct. People are naturally drawn to people who feel like them.

Bottled Enthusiasm

Act like a kid who has a secret they're so excited about that they can barely contain it. Not loud or over-the-top, but barely-contained excitement. Especially effective when you're about to show them their speed test results or reveal the promotion. If you're excited about what you have to offer, they become excited about hearing it.

13 — Handling

Smoke Screens

Smoke screens are unique in that they don't represent the real thoughts of the customer. They are things people say to get you off their doorstep so they don't have to deal with you. They typically come during the initial approach — while the customer barely knows anything about what you're selling. They are a reflexive response to being approached, not a genuine objection. The most common are "No thanks," "I'm not interested," and "I'm good."

The key to smoke screens is to treat them as almost nothing — because that's exactly what they are. The customer hasn't heard enough to have a real opinion yet. You want to say something that lets them know you heard them, but spend as little time addressing it as possible before continuing with your pitch. Do not stop. Do not apologize. Do not validate the smoke screen by treating it like a serious objection.

Whenever you receive a smoke screen, always smile and nod as they speak. Then respond with a bigger smile and the word "sure." You want it to seem as if you expected them to say exactly that. Do not shift your weight. Do not break eye contact. No deer in the headlights. Never, under any circumstances, repeat their concern back to them — ("Oh, you're not interested?") — this locks the smoke screen in and turns it into a real wall.

Responses — use in this order

First: "Sure, your internet isn't horrible, but why pay more for less?"

Second: "Sure, I'll just show you what I'm doing for your neighbors, that way at least you know what's going on."

Third: "Sure. You've lived here for a while… so you know how it gets during the evening or winter time with the slow internet and outages."

Use these phrases in order because simpler is always better. Only move to the second or third if the first doesn't hold them. Never use two smoke screen responses in one go — you will come across as desperate. One response, delivered with a smile and unwavering confidence, is almost always enough.

14 — Closing

Objections & The Sales Wheel

Objections

An objection is a real concern of the customer — something that is actually standing in the way of them buying. Unlike a smoke screen, which is reflexive, an objection is a genuine reason the customer has identified for not moving forward. Common objections include "I don't like contracts," "I need to talk to my spouse," "I want to shop around," and "I already have internet and it's fine." Customers will often have more than one.

Your initial reaction to any objection should be exactly like your reaction to a smoke screen — a big smile, head nods, and "sure." The worst thing you can do is react with visible concern, frustration, or defeat. The customer is watching your reaction as much as they're listening to your response.

Turn Negatives Into Positives: If done effectively, this immediately defuses tension. Use phrases like "That's actually the best thing about us" or "I'm glad you brought that up." Combined with a smile and a head nod, you're reframing the objection from a problem into a strength.

R.A.C. — Resolve, Ace, Close

R

Resolve

In order to ease the mind of the customer, they must have a logical reason why their concern is not going to be an issue. The resolve is different for every objection. Over time, learn at least three resolutions for each common objection so you never sound like a broken record. The resolve is not about winning an argument — it is about giving the customer the logical permission they need to move forward.

A

Ace

An ace is something that reminds the customer why they are buying and sweetens the deal — usually a free add-on or a price reduction. When you drop an ace, make it feel like it costs you something. Take a big deep breath, pause as if you're deciding, then commit: "Tell you what. I know you're going to love the service. Give me a shot while I'm already here with your neighbors, and I'll even throw in a $100 gift card — that way it's a no-brainer." The theatrics matter. If you casually toss in an incentive, it feels like a trick. If you make it feel like a genuine concession, it lands as a gift.

C

Close

After the ace, move immediately to a soft close. Read the customer and commit. Don't ask if they want to move forward — assume they do and start scheduling. "What day works better for you — Thursday or Friday?" is always more effective than "So do you want to sign up?"

The Sales Wheel

The Sales Wheel is a cycle, not a one-time play. A wheel revolves over and over again — and that's the point. You'll go through the wheel more than once in a single door interaction. Even genuinely interested customers raise objections before they commit, and the Sales Wheel gives you a structure for navigating that without losing your place or your confidence.

Build Value→ Resolve→ Acknowledge→ Close↻

These four stages repeat until the customer either buys or gives you a hard no that has been tested and confirmed. Your primary goal on every pass through the wheel is to build value. Maximize the positives of the service before you reach for a price drop or an ace. A customer who understands and believes in the value of fiber is a customer who stays installed and doesn't cancel. A customer who signed up only because you kept lowering the price is a chargeback waiting to happen.

Never drop the price on your first pass through the wheel. Wait until the second or third time through before you resort to price cuts. Dropping the price too soon looks suspicious — it signals that the original price was inflated. Start your pitch above the price floor so you always have somewhere to go. Do not over-explain. The depth of your resolve should match the seriousness of the objection. And always remember: the goal of the Sales Wheel is not to wear the customer down. It is to systematically remove the barriers between them and a decision they will be genuinely happy with.
